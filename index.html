Macro Context

October 2026 Market Overview

▼

October kept the Iran war risk premium in energy markets. More strikes hit Saudi pipelines and at least one crude vessel was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz. With the US mid-term elections now over, US strikes had slowed, but participants fear hostilities could re-escalate. US and Iranian negotiators continue to explore a phased Hormuz reopening with no target dates, and crude slipped slightly to begin November while staying volatile.

Firming: The propylene chain flipped to higher. Propylene settled up 1 cpp, Acetone up 1 cpp with 5-6 cpp increases announced for Nov 1, Ethylene up 1.5 cpp, and IPA and Propylene Oxide ticked up. MEK is rising globally and the main supplier raised prices 5 cpp at the end of October. Toluol & Xylol rose 3-4 cpp for November (about half of what was projected), while aliphatics and aromatics are stable to up but eased in early November as energy dropped. Methanol is firm on seasonal demand, low global inventories and Asia exports.

Still soft or easing: Propylene Glycols keep falling on better supply (imports hold the advantage), P-Series glycol ethers, MEA and plasticizers are soft, and E-Series EB/DB are soft despite a short-lived Ineos force majeure. Glycerin is loosening as major domestic producers return, imports climb and RV antifreeze demand has slowed. Methyl Soyate and tallow-based Oleic Acid eased, and corn futures have fallen sharply, pulling Ethanol off its highs.

Watch list: Ineos lifts its force majeure on glycol ethers 11/6, so watch what Eastman and Dow do about their 10 cpp increases. MEG/DEG/TEG are mixed: Asia MEG is correcting, DEG may free up, and TEG stays constrained. Florida's citrus crop is the smallest on record (10 million boxes), with Brazil pushing for an increase. Anti-dumping duties are tightening oleic acid imports, US Customs is reportedly withholding some glycerin imports, and trucking and ocean freight costs remain high. Ethanol 2027 contract pricing is coming soon.

Economy: The Fed held rates at its Oct 27-28 meeting after raising them in September. US consumer confidence improved as gasoline eased, but sentiment fell as inflation expectations rose. September new home sales fell 0.6% after August's gain, and a spike in 10-year Treasury yields continues to strain housing affordability. Demand is expected to slow further in November and December and to remain flat in 2027.

Ample availability also kept buyer activity cautious with consumers largely purchasing on an as-needed basis and showing limited urgency to secure incremental spot volumes. Despite the comfortable supply backdrop, higher propane and broader energy costs introduced greater volatility into the spot market during October and increased cost pressure on production economics. This cost-side support contributed to firmer pricing conditions even as underlying supply remained ample. October propylene contract prices ultimately settled above September levels, reinforcing the impact of higher production costs on the market. Participants say persistently elevated propane prices through Q1 '27 could continue to pressure producer economics and provide underlying support to higher propylene values. However, the extent of any upward price movement will likely depend on whether demand improves sufficiently to absorb the market's ample production. In the near term, the market remains shaped by competing fundamentals, with long supply and disciplined spot buying limiting upside potential while elevated feedstock and energy costs provide a firmer floor for pricing. US gasoline supplies declined towards the end of October as lower refinery rates reduced overall market availability. Refinery utilization fell by 0.6 percentage points at the end of October to 92.8%, signaling a modest slowdown in processing activity. Despite the decline in refinery runs, gasoline production increased during this same time, while consumption moved lower. The combination of higher production and weaker demand could limit the impact of the inventory drawing on market fundamentals, keeping near-term gasoline supply conditions relatively well supported. Biofuels producers are expected to remain strong through EOY. Soybean oil is the primary feedstock for MS. Domestic Sorbitol supply is healthy and contracted pricing is stable. Imported prices are stalled due to supply chain disruptions over the past few months, but domestic supply is capable of meeting demand. Tallow demand has been soft so Oleic Acid based on tallow has seen a healthy decrease for November. However, producers are predicting tallow demand will stay steady for the rest of 2026. Overall Oleic Acid supply is expected to be tight in the market due to imported oleic slowing down. Anti-dumping duties have been implemented on some importers causing supply to see tightness. As a result, some players predict this tightness to impact availability in the spot market, but that remains to be seen on slower demand. Prices for veg-based oleic acid are now disadvantaged over tallow-based. Participants feel this is a temporary price inversion and look for veg glycerin sources to be in better position by the new year. Supply is meeting softer demand moving into Q4.

⚠ War with Iran — Hormuz Reopening Talks Continue With No Target Dates

The war in the middle-east remains volatile. US and Iranian negotiators continue to explore a phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, but no target dates have been communicated. Strikes targeting Saudi pipelines and an attack on at least one crude vessel in the Strait kept crude elevated through October. US strikes slowed ahead of the US mid-term elections, and now that they are over, participants are watching for any re-escalation. The Strait and the Red Sea are still considered security choke points, driving freight costs and import delays. The global markets are fluid and can quickly change from one conversation or meeting to another.
