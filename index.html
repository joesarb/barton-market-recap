It appears the Memorandum of Understanding that was signed by the US and Iran on June 17 is now in jeopardy as recent strikes by both countries has dimmed hopes of a permanent peace deal. Markets have reacted with a small spike in energy prices that have started to retreat at the time of this writing. Please keep in mind tensions remain high as uncertainty lingers throughout the geopolitical landscape and the threats of ongoing war in the middle-east. The global markets are fluid and can quickly change from one conversation or meeting to another. Please continue to monitor your accounts for competitive activity where needed and communicate with your managers what you are seeing. This information is critical as we discuss market challenges with our channel partners.

Polymer Grade Propylene contract price settled down again in June by 8.5 cpp and is the second consecutive monthly decrease YTD. Prices were finalized on the last day of June following extensive negotiations between producers and buyers. Although the 45-day spot market average remained above the final settlement level, both sides ultimately reached an agreement allowing contracts to settle and providing greater pricing certainty for the market. The US and Iran signed the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) to end the conflict. Brokered by Pakistan and signed by President Trump and Iranian president Pezeshkian on June 17, the risk premium that has been for the last several months is now starting to soften. As a result. demand conditions are weighing on buying activity. Most consumers are not actively rebuilding propylene inventories as downstream demand remains subdued across key derivative markets including polypropylene. Purchasing activity has remained measured with buyers generally covering immediate spot requirements. Market participants have reported several production disruptions, including outages at both PDH units and steam crackers. However, these outages have had a limited impact on overall supply conditions. Product availability remains ample, supported by healthy inventories across all three propylene grades. Consequently, the market continues to be well supplied despite ongoing operational issues at select production facilities. Refinery utilization rates increased by .5 percentage points to 96.6% reflecting continued strong operating rates across the refining sector. EIA data also showed that gasoline supplied to the market, a widely used indicator of implied demand, increased at the end of June. While demand continued to recover during the reporting period, it remained below the level recorded during the same period in 2025. Propane inventories increased from the prior week as production rose and consumption also strenghtened. Propane remains the principal feedstock used in propylene production. Meanwhile, jet fuel inventories posted a weekly build at the end of June as both production and consumption declined during the period. US May construction spending was up .1% from April, down 1.5% year on year. US consumer confidence ticks up as lower oil prices ease inflation fears, also US consumer sentiment improves as gasoline prices moderate. US Q1 GDP growth estimate revised from 1.6% to 2.1% annual rate.
