Macro Context

September 2026 Market Overview

▼

September closed out Q3 with the Iran war past the six-month mark and the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea still treated as security choke points. US and Iranian negotiators are exploring a phased reopening of Hormuz, but traders doubt a deal is close. Houthi strikes on Saudi energy and the shutdown of Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline kept crude elevated, and producers announced a second round of price increases to start Q4.

Moving up: Energy-linked solvents lead the way. Light aliphatics (VM&P, Heptane, Hexane) are up over 8% for October, heavy aliphatics and aromatics face upward pressure on tight global diesel, and Toluol & Xylol formulated prices are expected to jump ~12 cpp. MEG/DEG/TEG are firming on Iran-driven supply constraints and an open export arbitrage, with DEG and TEG the tightest. Methanol is stable to up as Middle East trade flows have nearly stopped, and Glycerin stays tight with two regional producers sold out for 2026.

Still soft: Propylene settled down 2 cpp for September and its derivatives (PO, Acetone, IPA, PG, P-Series glycol ethers) keep trending down on weak demand and ample supply. Oxo alcohols are well supplied despite an Oct 1 producer increase. Ethylene settled down slightly with no cracker turnarounds scheduled for the rest of 2026, MEA is softening, and MEK erosion has slowed but demand is still soft. Expect competitive activity to build across most of these in Q4.

Watch list: One major producer's 12 cpp increase request on phenol and acetone (Benzene settled up sharply for September), a reported Gulf Coast Xylol production issue, one domestic DEG/TEG producer down since late July, and mid-October Florida citrus harvest figures for D-Limonene. Truck freight is tightening and hazmat ocean freight is escalating, while Section 301 tariffs from July are challenging imports.

Economy: The Fed raised rates 25 bps in mid-September, with another hike expected by year-end. The ISM Manufacturing PMI slipped to 54.6 in August from 55.6, still in expansion, led by plastics and rubber while chemicals lagged. US August industrial production was flat and chemicals were flat. New home sales rose 6.4% to an eight-month high, but a spike in 10-year Treasury yields threatens housing affordability. The US and China extended their trade truce to January 10, 2027. The early outlook for 2027 demand is mostly flat.

US MEG prices rose in September on higher Asian spot prices and global supply constraints as the continuing Iran conflict is the primary driver. While several products have corrected over the past month or so, renewed tensions and continued shipping constraints from the middle-east have prices back on the run up tracking crude oil. The feedstock Ethane saw prices slightly up, but continues to be the most stable NGL, showing limited exposure vs the significant volatility impacting crude oil and heavier NGL products. While Ethylene glycols corrected in July on indications of an improving Iran outlook, that changed at the end of July and into August and has worsened in September. The arbitrage is open for US exports just about anywhere. Domestically, large volume MEG is tight, but there is seemingly enough MEG available to service truck and rail as there is a glut of production in the US Gulf and demand remains flat. DEG, being a coproduct of MEG production, is tighter as total output is ~10-15% of produced MEG. Domestic DEG availability is further impacted as several N American producers are making up for production losses in other regions like the middle-east and parts of Asia. MEG is seeing less impact as the supply is in a better position. One domestic producer has been down since late July and might be down for several weeks, which removes significant spot DEG and TEG out of the Gulf. TEG tends to track DEG and on purpose TEG production is constrained due to lack of DEG. There are no imports of TEG heading to the US anytime soon. TEG is likely to continue moving up incrementally in price until the season peak demand hits. Overall, US supply is constrained and domestic sales volume is competing with the arbitrage value. Until product is flowing through the Strait of Hormuz more reliably, domestic MEG and DEG values will see risk of prices going up.

⚠ War with Iran — Past Six Months, Hormuz Reopening Talks Continue▼

The war in the middle-east has now passed six months and remains volatile. US and Iranian negotiators are exploring a phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, but traders are skeptical anything will get done soon, and Houthi strikes on Saudi energy continue to keep supply risks high. The Strait and the Red Sea are now considered security choke points, driving freight costs and import delays. The global markets are fluid and can quickly change from one conversation or meeting to another.
