Macro Context

⚠ War with Iran — Ongoing

Negotiations (to some degree) continue between the US and Iran for a long-term ceasefire. However, tensions remain high as uncertainty lingers throughout the geopolitical landscape and the threats of ongoing war in the middle-east. The global markets are taking it one day at a time knowing the situation is fluid and can quickly change from one conversation or meeting to another. But after several months of price hikes and global supply chain disruptions, the US is starting to see prices soften in some situations as demand slows and feed costs come down going into June. Please continue to monitor your accounts for competitive activity where needed and communicate with your managers what you are seeing. This information is critical as we discuss market challenges with our channel partners.

Polymer Grade Propylene contract price settled down 7 cpp for May, which is the first decrease in last five months. The market response to the US/Iran conflict has moderated slightly with crude oil prices falling below $100 per barrel as of this writing. Buying behavior remains cautious and primarily driven by immediate needs, while spot propylene prices dropped last month, prompting buyers to reassess their short-term outlook. Trading activity was subdued end of May with transactions occurring 10-12 cpp below where it did in April despite stable supply and steam cracker utilization above the ninety percent range. Refinery rates climbed to 94.5% making an increase of 2.9% second half of May indicating stronger processing activity across facilities. Meanwhile, crude oil inventories declined by 3.3 mm barrels over the same period according to data from the EIA (Energy Information Administration). This drawdown was smaller that market analysts had anticipated. The reduction in stockpiles coincided with higher refinery runs as increased throughput contributed to pulling more crude from storage, tightening overall supply levels modestly relative to recent historical averages in sector. US gasoline inventories declined end of May, reflecting sustained strength in demand. According to the EIA, gasoline stocks posted a reduction the last half of May, while product supplied to the market - a key proxy for implied demand - increased over the same period. On a four-week rolling basis, implied demand has returned to levels broadly in line with typical seasonal norms, signaling a stable consumption environment. Although weekly demand remained below the levels observed during the same period in 2025, the overall trend points to a recovery in consumption. The four-week average of gasoline supplied to the market stood at approximately 8.9 mm barrels per day, largely unchanged from the corresponding period last year. In other refined product markets, propane inventories declined end of May as both production and consumption decreased. Meanwhile, jet inventories increased over the same period, supported by higher production levels and a concurrent decline in consumption. US consumer confidence falls on price shocks from middle-east conflict. US consumer sentiment falls for third month on high gasoline prices and inflation fears and US April housing starts fall 2.8% from March while single-family home starts plunge 9%. Colorado State University released its initial 2026 Atlantic hurricane season forecast on April 9. The outlook predicts levels of named storms, hurricanes, major hurricanes and accumulated cyclone energy with updated forecasts scheduled for June 10, July 8 and August 5.

Full Detail
Methyl Soyate prices continue to be supported by bullish RVO policy and higher energy prices. Prices were up 7% to start June. Supply remains in good standing as we near the back half of Q2. Soybean oil is the primary feedstock for MS. Domestic Sorbitol supply is healthy and contracted pricing for 2026 was a rollover from 2025. Imported prices are stalled due to supply chain disruptions over the past few months, but domestic supply is capable of meeting demand. Oleic Acid supply continues to be tight and prices rose again for June, up over 10%. The feedstock tallow is seeing strong demand in the production of bio-fuels as they gear up to meet additional RVO policy for 2026. In addition, there is less beef in the market where tallow is sourced. There is a lot tugging on tallow right now, which is supporting the price hikes in the market. Logistical fuel surcharges are also having an impact on oleic acid business with the huge jump in energy charges. Our main channel partner is introducing a veg-based oleic acid that Barton is going to test. If successful, there will be a price savings to tallow based.
