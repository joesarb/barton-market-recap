Macro Context

August 2026 Market Overview

▼

August ended with fresh volatility: the US and Iran exchanged missile strikes at the end of the month, trade through the Strait of Hormuz stalled after the attacks, and energy prices turned up again to begin September. Diplomatic talks continue to fail, which keeps the geopolitical risk premium in crude. If hostilities continue for a week or two, producers are expected to announce price increases for the middle of September.

Energy-linked products firming: Aliphatics and Aromatics are stable to up. Heavier aliphatics and aromatics had increases announced for 9/1 and another is possible mid-month, and Toluol & Xylol index prices settled up going into September. Asian MEG spot prices are at their highest in years, and DEG and TEG are tight, with one domestic producer down since late July. Refined glycerin stays tight through October on RV antifreeze demand and low imports.

Still soft: Propylene contract prices fell and spot hit new lows, and the derivatives followed: Propylene Oxide fell, Acetone settled down 2 cpp, IPA was down, and MEK keeps eroding. Propylene Glycols are in better supply with Lyondell's Pasadena facility fully operational. P-Series and E-Series glycol ethers, MEA and plasticizers remain soft on weak demand.

Watch list: logistics are getting harder (fewer truck drivers, fewer vessels, typhoon activity in Asia and Panama Canal congestion, with the Canal cutting transit slots in September on El Niño). Florida's citrus season starts in late November with only two processors. BASF's European force majeure still cuts off DPHP supply. In the news: Olin and Huntsman shareholders approved an all-stock merger, Shell's planned sale of its US chemicals business has drawn interest from ExxonMobil, Lyondell and Kuwait Petroleum, and the US imposed 50% tariffs on some Canadian imports with Canada retaliating.

Economy: US consumer confidence fell in August on a more pessimistic outlook for business conditions and the labor market. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh signaled a potential rate hike later this year to curb inflation. July durable goods orders rose 1.1%, July PCE inflation was up 3.7% year over year, Q2 GDP growth was confirmed at a 1.5% annual rate, and new home sales fell 10.5% from June to July. Despite weak employment and retail data, AI and high-tech investment continue to support growth, and the early outlook for 2027 demand is mostly flat.

US MEG prices have remained stable going into September. While several products in the PET chem family have reduced prices over the last few months, shipping constraints from the middle-east continue to impact ethylene glycols. The ethylene glycol price correction in late June and early July on indications of an improving US/Iran outlook has changed in the last month as energy prices are on the rise again. Outside of the US and China, feedstocks are short and in turn, shorting the derivative value chain. MEG Asian spot prices, which drive the global market, are up significantly and at its highest level in years on lower inventories in China. Both MEG and DEG are at historical lows in China, however slow demand is keeping prices from moving too much. Domestically, large volume MEG is tight, but there is seemingly enough available to service truck and rail needs as there is a glut of production in the US Gulf and demand remains flat. Keep in mind domestic contract MEG prices will move with China ASP and domestic producers will likely announce increases to the spot market if this continues. DEG being a coproduct of MEG production is tighter as total output is ~10-15% of produced MEG. Domestic DEG availability is further impacted as several N American producers are making up for production losses in other regions like the middle-east and parts of Asia. MEG is seeing less impact as the supply is in a better position. One domestic producer has been down since late July and might be down for several weeks, which removes significant spot DEG and TEG out of the Gulf. TEG trends to track DEG and on purpose TEG production is constrained due to lack of DEG. There are no imports of TEG heading to the US anytime soon. TEG is likely to continue moving up incrementally in price until the season peak demand hits. Overall, US supply is constrained and domestic sales volume is competing with the arbitrage value. Until product is flowing through the Strait of Hormuz more reliably, domestic MEG and DEG values will see risk of prices going up.
